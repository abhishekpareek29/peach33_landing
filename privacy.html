Privacy policy

This policy explains what personal data the Peach app, the service behind it and the peach33.com website collect, why, who we share it with, how long we keep it, and the choices you have.

Peach is in an invite-only beta, distributed through Apple TestFlight. This policy describes the kinds of data Peach handles and how, both for the features Peach has today and for new features that handle data in the same ways. Before a feature handles your data in a new way, we update this policy.

In short

  • We collect your account details, what you share with Peach and what Peach does for you, and use them only to provide and protect Peach.
  • Peach’s AI runs on Microsoft Azure. The app asks for your permission before your content is sent there. Neither we nor Microsoft Azure train AI models on your content.
  • Notifications, location sharing and connected services, such as Google Calendar, are off until you turn them on.
  • The app has no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting tools, and does not track you. We do not sell your personal information.
  • Your data is stored by Fly.io in the United States. The companies that handle it for us are listed on our Service providers page.
  • You can delete your account in the app at any time.

Who we are

Peach is operated by Peach33 (“we”, “us”), a sole proprietorship based in San Francisco, California, USA. We decide how and why your personal data is processed, which makes us the data controller.

This policy covers the Peach app, the online service it connects to, and the website at peach33.com. Questions go to support@peach33.com.

Data we collect

We collect the kinds of data below. The features named are examples: a new feature that uses the same kind of data in the same way is covered by this policy, and for one that does not, we update this policy first.

Account details

  • Your email address and password. We store the password only as a salted bcrypt hash, never the password itself. Where the app offers it, we email you codes to verify your address or reset your password.
  • An optional display name, your time zone, and account status, such as when the account was created.
  • If you sign in with another account, such as Google, where the app offers it, the details that account shares with us to sign you in, such as your email address.
  • Sign-up is by invitation. For each invitation we keep the invited email address and a hashed copy of the invitation code.

What you share, and what Peach does for you

  • Conversations. Your messages, Peach’s replies (including the working notes the AI produces while answering), conversation titles, and summaries Peach writes to keep track of long conversations.
  • Memories. Short facts Peach saves about you so it can remember them, each with a link to the conversation it came from.
  • Routines and reminders. What to do and when, each run’s full transcript, which is stored like a conversation, its results, and the notifications Peach sends you.
  • Documents. Files you upload so Peach can answer questions about them. We keep the file’s name, type, size and a fingerprint (hash), its text, and passages with their embeddings: lists of numbers that let Peach find the passages relevant to a question. We do not keep the original file.
  • What Peach reads for you. Web pages, search results, weather reports, images it describes, and information from services you connect. What Peach reads becomes part of your conversation.

Settings, approvals and activity

  • Settings. The name and look you choose for Peach, your progress through setup, which abilities Peach may use, and which kinds of actions Peach must ask you about first.
  • Approvals. The actions Peach asked you to approve, and your answers.
  • Activity records. A log of the tools Peach used and the steps it took for each request, including the content it worked with, such as the AI’s working text, the web addresses and commands its tools carried, and what they returned, and a record of actions Peach took without asking, so you can review them and undo some of them.

Your approximate location

Location sharing is off on each of your devices unless you turn it on there and allow the app to use your location while you are using it. It lets Peach answer questions about the weather and places near you.

  • What the app sends. Only while you are using the app, never in the background: your phone’s position (approximate or precise, depending on what you allow), how accurate it is, when it was read, and the names of the place you are in: city or town, state or region, and country. iOS sends the position to Apple’s geocoding service to find these names. The app sends a reading when you open it, and again after you have moved or the last one has aged.
  • What we keep. Only your latest location: one per account, the most recently read from any of your devices, never a history. We round the coordinates to 2 decimal places (about 1 km) before storing them, with the accuracy, when the position was read and received, and the place names.
  • How Peach uses it. Peach, including your routines, sees the place names and how long ago the position was read, never the coordinates. Web searches and weather lookups use the place names, and a weather lookup for where you are in the United States can use the rounded coordinates (see Who we share data with).
  • Turning it off. Turn location sharing off in Settings, Location. We then delete your location and keep only the time you turned it off, so that a reading taken before then is ignored. The setting applies only to the device where you change it: another of your devices where sharing is still on keeps sending readings. If you withdraw the app’s location permission in your phone’s settings, the app turns sharing off the next time you open it. Turning sharing off does not delete messages or memories that mention a place.
  • Signing out stops the readings from that device. The location we hold stays until a newer reading replaces it, you turn sharing off or you delete your account. It is never deleted automatically, whatever its age.

Connected services

You can connect other services to Peach in Settings, Connectors. Connecting one is your choice: you sign in to that service and approve what Peach may access, and Peach uses that access only to answer your requests and run your routines.

  • Google Calendar. Google’s permission lets Peach view the events on your calendars, but Peach reads only your primary calendar: each event’s title, start and end times, time zone, location and link. It cannot add, change or delete events. The events Peach reads for a request become part of your conversation and of that request’s activity record. We store the access Google grants, encrypted, and keep no other copy of your calendar. If a routine watches your calendar for changes, Google tells our service when something changes, and we keep what we need to receive those notices.

To disconnect a service, remove Peach’s access in your account with that service, such as your Google Account’s security settings, or email us. Deleting your Peach account also disconnects it. When you disconnect through us or delete your account, we ask the service to revoke Peach’s access and delete the access we stored.

Peach’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • we use Google user data only to provide and improve the features you use it for in Peach;
  • we do not use it to serve advertisements, and we do not sell it;
  • we transfer it to others only as needed to provide those features, such as to Microsoft Azure to generate Peach’s responses, for security, to comply with the law, or, only with your explicit prior consent, as part of a merger or acquisition;
  • we do not use it to train AI models, and Microsoft Azure does not either;
  • we do not let people read it unless you ask us to, it is needed for security or to comply with the law, or it is aggregated and anonymized for internal operations.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, the app sends us the push token Apple assigns to the app on your phone, plus an ID for this installation of the app. Push notifications carry only a fixed message for their kind, such as “Peach replied”, with the notification’s ID and a link into the app, never the content of your conversations.

Technical and usage data

  • Sign-in sessions. The app holds short-lived access tokens and longer-lived refresh tokens. We store refresh tokens only as hashes.
  • Usage records. For each AI request we record which model was used and why, the number of tokens, whether it succeeded, how long it took, and internal reference numbers. These records contain no message content. We use them to run usage limits and to understand costs.
  • Abuse protection. To limit repeated attempts and requests, we keep short-lived counters keyed by a keyed hash of your IP address, email address or account reference number, and we count your daily use of Peach’s abilities. We do not store the IP address itself in our database.
  • Server logs. Our service logs technical events: times, request and account reference numbers, status codes, timings and token counts. Our main service’s logs are designed to leave out message content, locations and place names, and to replace IP addresses and requested addresses with placeholders. Error logs may occasionally include fragments of content. Our hosting provider, Fly.io, handles your IP address at its network edge to deliver traffic to us.
  • Operational records. Records of operations on your account, such as setting up its services or deleting it, and a log of administrative actions taken on the service.

Where your assistant runs

Your assistant runs inside our service on Fly.io, which serves every account. Your data is kept in our central database, also hosted by Fly.io, and is linked to your account. Nothing you share with Peach is shown to other users.

Phone permissions, and what the app does not collect

The app asks for a phone permission only when you use a feature that needs it: notifications; your location, if you turn on location sharing; and the microphone and speech recognition, used only while you dictate a message. Dictation is turned into text on your phone: no audio is stored or sent, by the app or to Apple, and we receive only the text you send. The app does not access your contacts, photos or camera, and apart from the push token, it reads no advertising identifier or other device identifier. Apart from the technical information any internet connection carries, it does not send your device model, operating system version or language settings to us. It contains no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting tools. If you use TestFlight, Apple shares crash reports and feedback with us, which include your device model and iOS version.

When you contact us

If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you write, and use them to reply.

Data stored on your device

The app keeps a small amount of data in the iOS Keychain, which iOS protects. These items are available only while your device is unlocked, stay on that device and are not synced to iCloud:

  • your sign-in tokens;
  • messages you have written that have not yet reached our servers, plus delivery receipts (the text is removed once the server accepts a message);
  • reference numbers of requests and approvals the app is still tracking;
  • records of your choices, each with your account’s reference number: that you accepted the AI notice, and whether you share your location, whether you have answered the app’s offer to share it, and whether the app still has to ask us to delete it. They never contain your position or place names;
  • an ID for this installation of the app, and whether you have answered the app’s offer to turn on notifications. It contains nothing about your account.

Your conversations, memories, routines and settings are loaded from our servers when you use the app and are not saved to your device’s storage.

Signing out removes your sign-in tokens. Messages that never reached our servers and the records of your choices stay on the device for that account, and the installation ID stays for the app. Deleting your account also tries to remove those unsent messages and tracking data, but leaves the records of your choices and the installation ID. iOS may keep Keychain items after you delete the app.

AI processing by Microsoft Azure

Peach uses AI models run by Microsoft Azure to understand your requests and write its replies. For each request, we send Microsoft Azure what it needs to answer:

  • your message and the recent conversation, including earlier summaries;
  • your saved memories, your assistant’s name, the current time and your time zone, your routines when relevant, and, if you share your location, the place names and how long ago the position was read, never the coordinates;
  • the content a task needs, such as passages from your documents, web pages, search results, weather reports, events from services you connect, and images Peach describes;
  • passages from documents you upload, so Azure can create their embeddings.

We do not include your account email address or password in these requests.

Microsoft processes this content as our service provider to generate Peach’s responses. Microsoft does not use this content to train or improve AI models or share it with OpenAI. It checks it automatically for misuse and may store flagged content for review by authorized Microsoft staff. We do not ask Azure to store your conversations. We do not train AI models on your content.

Before you first chat with Peach, the app tells you about this and asks you to agree to sending this content to Microsoft Azure. Your answer is stored only on your device. Peach cannot work without this processing: if you do not agree, sign out. To stop this processing later, stop using Peach and delete your account.

Peach’s replies are generated automatically. We do not use automated decision-making that has legal or similarly significant effects on you.

How we use data

  • To provide Peach: to create and run your account and assistant, answer your messages, remember what you asked it to, run your routines, search your documents, use the services you connect and, if you share your location, answer questions about the weather and places near you.
  • To keep the service secure and prevent abuse, including sign-in limits and daily usage limits.
  • To operate, troubleshoot and improve the reliability of the service.
  • To respond to you when you contact us, and to tell you about important changes to the beta.
  • To comply with the law and protect our rights and the safety of others.

We do not use your data for advertising, we do not sell it, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising.

Who we share data with

We share data only with the companies that run Peach for us, and only what each needs. By what they do:

  • AI models: Microsoft Azure, as described above.
  • Hosting: Fly.io hosts our service, which runs your assistant, and our database and its backups.
  • Web search and weather: Brave Search runs Peach’s web searches and weather lookups; if we use Microsoft’s web search for Azure instead, Microsoft Bing runs the searches. Brave receives the search terms Peach writes and, so that results are local, the city, region and country of your last known location if you share your location, or otherwise just the country of your time zone. The US National Weather Service (api.weather.gov) answers weather lookups for places in the United States and receives their approximate coordinates, including your stored location’s rounded coordinates when you ask about the weather where you are.
  • Apple: TestFlight distributes the beta, the Apple Push Notification service delivers push notifications if you allow them, and Apple’s geocoding service names the place you are in if you share your location.
  • Email: Cloudflare forwards the emails you send to support@peach33.com, and the waitlist sign-ups Formspree emails us, to our inbox. Our email providers host that inbox and send the emails we send you.
  • Website: GitHub, with its content delivery network Fastly, serves peach33.com, and Formspree runs its waitlist.

Our Service providers page lists these companies, what each does and what it receives. We update it when a provider changes.

Microsoft Azure processes data on our behalf under data-processing terms that require it to protect the data at least as well as this policy describes. Fly.io hosts our service under its terms of service, which require it to keep our data confidential and protect it with reasonable safeguards. The other providers, including Microsoft for Bing searches, handle data under their own service and privacy terms.

Services you connect, such as Google, provide data to Peach under their own terms and receive Peach’s requests for it. Websites that Peach reads for you, search providers and the US National Weather Service receive requests from our servers, so they see our server’s address rather than yours.

We may also disclose data if the law requires it, or where needed to protect the rights, property or safety of our users, the public or us. If Peach33 is transferred to someone else, your data would go with it under this policy, and we would tell you first; data we received from Google would go only with your explicit prior consent.

Nothing you share with Peach is shown to other users.

Data the website collects

Waitlist

If you join the waitlist on peach33.com, your email address is sent to Formspree, the form service we use. Like any web request, Formspree also receives your IP address, browser information and the address of the page you came from. Formspree emails each sign-up to our inbox. We use your email address only to invite you to Peach or tell you when your spot opens. If you submit the form with JavaScript turned off, Formspree handles it on its own pages and may show a Google reCAPTCHA check. We keep your waitlist email until we invite you or you ask to be removed. To be removed from the waitlist, email us.

Fonts and hosting

The site serves its own fonts, so no font service receives your IP address. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, served through Fastly’s network; GitHub logs visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes. Cloudflare, which provides peach33.com’s DNS, may also pass the site’s traffic on to GitHub to protect it from attacks, and then handles visitors’ IP addresses too.

No cookies or trackers

The website sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser, and uses no analytics or tracking tools. If Cloudflare needs to check that a visit is not automated, it may set a security cookie. Neither the website nor the app tracks you across other websites or apps, so they work the same whether or not your browser sends a “Do Not Track” signal.

How long we keep data

  • Your account, settings and content (conversations, memories, routines and documents): until you delete them or delete your account. These do not expire automatically.
  • Notifications: notices that Peach replied or asked you a question are deleted 30 days after they are created; other notifications are kept until you delete your account.
  • Approvals: your answer and when you gave it are kept until you delete your account. For a request made in a chat, the action’s details are erased 7 days after the request finishes.
  • Working copies of messages: removed 7 days after your assistant finishes with the message.
  • Activity records: the log of tools and steps is kept for 30 days. A record of an action Peach took without asking keeps its details for up to 30 days, or 7 days for a request made in a chat, and then only which tool was used, the outcome and when, until you delete your account. Both are erased sooner if you delete the conversation or message that started the work.
  • Location: only the latest is kept, whatever its age, until a newer reading replaces it, you turn location sharing off or you delete your account. After you turn sharing off, we keep only the time you did so, until you delete your account.
  • Connected services: the access we store is kept until you disconnect the service through us or delete your account. If you remove Peach’s access in your account with that service instead, we delete the stored access the next time Peach tries to use it.
  • Sign-in sessions: refresh tokens expire after 30 days and are revoked when you sign out; their records are deleted with your account.
  • Push tokens: deleted when you sign out, when Apple reports that a token is no longer valid, or with your account. If the app can’t reach us when you sign out, the token is deleted later: when Apple reports it is no longer valid, when the app registers again on that phone, or with your account. Copies of the token kept with notification delivery records are removed at the same time.
  • Abuse-protection counters: deleted about a day after their time window ends.
  • Usage records: kept for cost and abuse tracking, with no set deletion date. They never contain message content, and when you delete your account they are unlinked from it.
  • Operational records: records of operations on your account, and the log of administrative actions, are kept after account deletion, with no set deletion date. They contain your former account’s reference number but no email, name or content.
  • Server logs: kept by Fly.io for 7 days.
  • Backups: our database is backed up daily and each backup is kept for 5 days, so deleted data can remain in backups for up to about 5 days.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account in the app under Settings, Delete account. Deletion is available to everyone, wherever you live.

What happens right away

Your account is disabled and you are signed out on every device. Your sign-in sessions are revoked, your routines stop, queued messages and pending notifications are cleared, your location is erased, and outstanding invitations for your email are revoked.

What happens next

In the background, we ask the services you connected to revoke Peach’s access, then permanently delete your account record and everything linked to it: conversations, memories, routines, notifications, uploaded documents, settings, approvals, activity records, connected-service access, sessions and push tokens. If a step fails, your account stays disabled and signed out. We retry automatically, and if that does not work, we finish the deletion manually. Email us if you would like confirmation that it is complete.

What remains

  • Backups, for up to about 5 days.
  • Usage records with no content, unlinked from your account.
  • Operational records that contain your former account’s reference number only.
  • Server logs, for the period described above.
  • On your device, the records of your choices and the installation ID described in Data stored on your device.

Deleting individual items

You can delete memories, routines and your own messages in the app. To delete a whole conversation or a document you uploaded without deleting your account, email us. When we delete a conversation, we erase its messages, title and summaries; basic records such as timestamps remain until you delete your account.

Security

  • The app talks to our service only over HTTPS.
  • Passwords are stored as salted bcrypt hashes. Refresh tokens and invitation codes are stored as hashes.
  • The access that connected services grant Peach is stored encrypted.
  • Our database is stored on a disk that Fly.io encrypts at rest.
  • Our logs are designed to exclude message content; error logs may occasionally include fragments of it.

Your conversations, memories and document text are stored in our database in readable form so Peach can use them; they are not end-to-end encrypted. As the operator, we have administrative access to this database. We access it only when needed to operate, troubleshoot, secure or support the service, or when the law requires it. People read data we received from Google only as described in Connected services.

No system is perfectly secure. If we learn of a security incident that affects your personal data, we will notify you as the law requires.

Your choices and rights

In the app

  • Turn Peach’s abilities on or off, and choose which kinds of actions Peach must ask you about first.
  • View and delete memories and routines, and delete your own messages.
  • Turn notifications and location sharing on or off, and connect services. Turning location sharing off deletes your location from our servers.
  • Sign out, or delete your account.

By email

Wherever you live, you can ask us to:

  • give you a copy of your data in a machine-readable format;
  • correct inaccurate data;
  • delete specific data, or your whole account;
  • restrict or object to how we use your data.

Email support@peach33.com from the address on your account. We may need to confirm the request is yours. We will respond within one month and will not charge you, unless a request is clearly unfounded or excessive.

If you are in California

We do not sell your personal information or share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, and we do not disclose it to third parties for their own direct marketing. You can review your information in the app, and ask us by email to correct it.

If you are in the EU or UK

We rely on these legal bases:

  • Contract: to provide Peach under our Terms, including sending content to Microsoft Azure so Peach can answer you. The screen in the app that asks you to agree to this is a notice Apple requires; it is not a separate legal basis.
  • Consent: to use your waitlist email. You can withdraw consent at any time by emailing us; this does not affect processing that already happened.
  • Consent: to use your location, if you turn on location sharing. You can withdraw consent at any time by turning sharing off on each of your devices; this does not affect processing that already happened.
  • Legitimate interests: to keep the service secure, prevent abuse, and keep usage, log and operational records.
  • Legal obligation: where the law requires us to keep or disclose data.

You need to give us your email address and password to create an account, and Peach cannot answer you without the AI processing described above. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

Peach is not directed at children and is not for anyone under 18. If we learn that we have collected personal data from someone under that age, or from a child under 13, we will delete the account and its data. If you believe a child is using Peach, email us.

Where data is processed

We are based in San Francisco, California, USA. Our service and database run on Fly.io in the United States (Ashburn, Virginia). Microsoft Azure processes AI requests in the United States (Virginia), and may process a request in another Azure region to manage capacity. If you use Peach from another country, your data is transferred to and processed in these places. Microsoft and Fly.io take part in the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, including its UK Extension, which covers transfers of personal data from the EU and UK to them. Microsoft’s data processing terms also include the Standard Contractual Clauses.

Changes to this policy

We update this policy before Peach handles your data in a new way, such as collecting a new kind of data, using it for a new purpose or sharing it with a new kind of recipient. We keep the list of companies that handle data for us on our Service providers page, which we update when a provider changes. We will post any changes to this policy on this page and update the date at the top. If a change materially affects how we handle your data, we will tell you before it takes effect, through the app, the TestFlight release notes or by email, and ask for your permission again where the law or Apple requires it.
